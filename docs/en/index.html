The camera follows
whoever is speaking.

An Adobe Premiere Pro panel that listens to your podcast, works out who is talking at each moment and cuts the timeline to the right camera. It works even with a single microphone for everyone.

The work it takes off your hands

Editing a multi-camera podcast is mostly repetition: listen, find who is talking, cut, repeat for an hour of footage.

Cuts

Switches to the speaker

Each person has their own camera. AutoCut cuts to them when they start talking — and lands the cut slightly early, the way an editor would.

Breathes

Uses the wide shot

When two people talk over each other, or when a close-up has been held too long, it goes to the wide camera.

Flags

Marks what it is unsure about

If someone seems to be talking while the camera is on another person, it leaves a red marker on the sequence so you only review that spot.

Speaks your language

English and Portuguese

The whole panel switches language from a selector in the corner — error messages and the markers it leaves on the sequence included.

Two ways to record

Pick in the panel how your podcast was recorded. Everything else works the same.

Default mode

A single microphone

One audio track for everyone. The sound tells it when someone speaks; the mouth movement in each camera tells it who. It measures lips, jaw and chin — and, above all, whether that movement follows the rhythm of the syllables in the voice. Someone who is only reacting, smiling or laughing silently moves their mouth out of that rhythm and does not steal the camera.

Alternative

One microphone per person

Each person on their own audio channel. It learns how much of each voice bleeds into the other microphones and subtracts that before deciding — so a loud host does not take the camera while the guest is the one talking.

Why it does not get stuck on one person

The most annoying failure in automatic switching is the camera sticking to the host while the guest talks. AutoCut was built around that problem.

  • It decides the whole conversation at once. Instead of judging moment by moment and holding the previous camera whenever it is unsure, it picks the sequence of cuts that best explains the entire conversation, with a cost for every switch.
  • Short lines go to whoever has the stronger evidence, not automatically to whoever was already on air.
  • An “uh-huh” followed by silence does not become a shot. It counts actual speaking time inside the segment, not just its length.
  • A turned head no longer fools it. Mouth measurements are taken along the face’s own axis rather than across the mouth’s width: turning sideways shrinks that width, which used to make someone “look like they were talking” just by moving their head.
  • Whatever stayed doubtful is handed to you marked, instead of hiding the mistake inside an hour of video.

Install

macOS, Premiere Pro 2022 or newer. It needs ffmpeg — and, for the single-microphone mode, an isolated Python with MediaPipe (around 300 MB, installed in its own folder).

  1. Get the project

    git clone https://github.com/Vargaseo/autocut.git
cd autocut

  2. Install the panel

    Copies the panel into Premiere’s extensions folder and allows panels without an Adobe signature.

    bash install.sh

  3. Set up mouth reading

    Only for the single-microphone mode. Creates the isolated Python and downloads the face model.

    bash setup_lipsync.sh

  4. Open it in Premiere

    Quit and reopen Premiere, then go to Window › Extensions (legacy) › AutoCut.

How to use it

  1. Read the sequence

    The panel lists your tracks. You confirm each person’s camera and which one is the wide shot.

  2. Analyse

    It reads the files and shows a colour preview of the shots. From then on, changing a setting updates the preview instantly, with no new analysis.

  3. Apply the cuts

    It duplicates the sequence and cuts on the copy. Cameras that are off air are disabled, not deleted — to change a cut by hand, just enable or disable the clip with Shift+E.

What it does not do yet

  • It does not read nested or merged clips. Use the original files on the tracks.
  • A face fully turned away or covered cannot be read. In those stretches it works out the speaker from the other cameras, which is solid with two people and shakier with four. A face in profile reads normally.
  • Audio with changed speed is not supported, and video tracks must be unlocked.
  • There is no Adobe digital signature. The installer turns on PlayerDebugMode, the Premiere mode that accepts unsigned panels — and it applies to every panel, not just this one. You can turn it back off with defaults write com.adobe.CSXS.12 PlayerDebugMode 0.
  • Only tested on macOS. The panel is built on technology that runs on both systems, but nobody has run it on Windows yet.

The panel does not use the internet: it reads your sequence’s files with ffmpeg and Python on your own machine, and sends nothing anywhere.

Work in progress. The decision logic is covered by automated tests, but with simulated audio and video — it has not been measured on real recordings yet. Treat the first pass as a rough cut and check the red markers.
